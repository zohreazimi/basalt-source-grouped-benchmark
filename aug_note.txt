Synthetic analyses were added to the training folds so that every class had at least 1,500 samples. They were generated in log-concentration space, and all ratios were recomputed from them; major oxides were not re-closed. Five schemes were compared: (i) plain duplication, as a control for the change in class balance; (ii) analytical noise, a real analysis perturbed by log-normal noise with a standard deviation of 2% for major oxides and 8% for trace elements; (iii) nearest-neighbour interpolation (SMOTE; Chawla et al., 2002) between an analysis and one of its five nearest same-class neighbours, ln **x**_{new} = λ ln **x**_{a} + (1 − λ) ln **x**_{b} with λ uniform on (0, 1); (iv) the same interpolation with a partner drawn from the same reference code; and (v) with a partner drawn from a different reference code (cross-source mixing). Schemes (iv) and (v) are weighted geometric means of two compositions, not models of magma mixing. The target and the noise levels were set before the runs reported here and were not tuned; targets of 750 and 3,000 and a variant with one noisy copy of every training sample were run afterwards. Each scheme was run with three seeds for LightGBM, and changes relative to the unaugmented model are averaged over seeds within the bootstrap.
